Automatic Speech Recognition in CALL

Trinity College Dublin Hilary Term 2026 · Speech technologies

Summary: This research note reviews claims about ASR-supported pronunciation practice, feedback, and learner experience, and argues for combining automated practice with human interaction.

Over the past five years, there has been a significant shift in CALL, driven by the rapid evolution of ASR. While in its early days, ASR was a rather cumbersome instrument that failed to recognize non-native pronunciation, today, thanks to advances in machine learning, ASR is an advanced system that can provide instant feedback on pronunciation skills. This technology now enables the transition from mere vocabulary study to spoken fluency.

A Decade of Progress in Ten Minutes

When we look at the background of this technology, the progress is staggering. Recent syntheses of the field, such as the Aggregating the Evidence of Automatic Speech Recognition Research Claims in CALL, suggest that we are finally moving past experimental trials toward a consistent understanding of how ASR impacts pronunciation. Over the past decade—and specifically within the last five years—research has shifted from asking "Does it work?" to "How can we make it most effective for the learner?" The consensus is emerging: ASR is not just a novelty; it is a robust tool for assessing and ultimately improving second-language (L2) pronunciation.

From Lab Experiments to Classroom Reality

Another important trend in recent times is the trend towards intelligent feedback. Modern technology no longer merely gives a simple "pass/fail" verdict on pronunciation. It pinpoints exact phoneme-based mistakes made by users. As noted in a Systematic Literature Review of Research on Automatic Speech Recognition in EFL Pronunciation, such technologies have been used in the context of English as a foreign language (EFL) learning. The paper stresses that using ASR provides learners with high-frequency exposure to the target language, which cannot be achieved through classroom instruction when one instructor works with 30 learners at once.

The Human Element: Hybrid Models

Perhaps the most interesting development in the last five years is the realization that ASR works best when it complements human interaction rather than replacing it. Researchers have begun exploring mixed-methods approaches to see how students actually feel about these tools. In a Mixed Methods Investigation into the Impact of ASR on EFL Learners, findings suggest that while ASR provides the "safe" environment needed for repetitive practice, combining it with peer correction creates a more holistic learning experience. This study shows that ASR technology significantly reduces "foreign language anxiety," allowing students to make mistakes privately before performing in front of their peers or instructors.

The Future is Audible

As we move into the next five years, ASR integration in CALL will be even smoother. From here on out, there won't be any need for dedicated pronunciation applications, since each digital platform, whether mobile or VR, will hear the learner and respond to their input. With a solid foundation established through research such as the above, we can be sure that our language-learning revolution through voice will be not only technologically but also pedagogically sound. We are no longer in the era when CALL was only about reading and writing in a new language; now it is about speaking as well.
